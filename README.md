## 🎯 Mission Statement
Our mission is to advance academic equity for commuter students by transforming underutilized campus infrastructure into accessible, collaborative learning environments. We champion student wellbeing by eliminating the daily friction of finding a workspace, ensuring every student has a place to belong and focus on campus.

---

## 🧩 Problem Statement

Over 60% of UBC Vancouver’s 60,000+ students commute to campus ([AMS Academic Experience Survey](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/)), frequently navigating schedules with awkward 50-minute to 3-hour gaps between lectures ([Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)). During peak hours (10:00 AM – 4:00 PM), dedicated study hubs such as the Irving K. Barber Learning Centre, Koerner Library, and the AMS Nest routinely reach full capacity, while bookable group rooms are reserved weeks in advance ([Cha & Kim, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acalib.2015.03.014); [Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)). Paradoxically, this space shortage is an information and coordination problem rather than a physical lack of rooms ([Beyrouthy et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1057/palgrave.jors.2602523)). Although [UBC’s Official Classroom Usage Guidelines](https://learningspaces.ubc.ca/useful-resources/guidelines/) explicitly permit students to use general teaching classrooms as study spaces whenever classes or events are not in session (until 11:00 PM or building lock-up), dozens of unlocked departmental classrooms sit completely empty at any given hour because students cannot easily discover them ([Beyrouthy et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1057/palgrave.jors.2602523); [Beckers et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2015.1123230)).

The inability to secure a reliable workspace directly undermines student productivity, wellbeing, and equitable access to education ([Pokorny et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-016-0063-3); [Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)). Commuter students who cannot practically return home are left wandering buildings in search of an open seat and a power outlet, or settling for noisy hallway floors and stairwells ([Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)). This daily scavenger hunt wastes hours of recovery and study time every week, increases physical fatigue, and disproportionately penalizes students who lack the privilege of on-campus residence rooms or exclusive departmental lounge access ([Pokorny et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-016-0063-3)). When students attempt to collaborate on team projects between classes, the problem compounds: coordinating overlapping free windows alongside a mutually accessible, unoccupied room requires tedious back-and-forth guesswork ([Beckers et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2015.1123230); [Harrop & Turpin, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/13614533.2013.740961)).

Current student workarounds are highly inefficient and unreliable for day-to-day use ([Azizi et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2020.107355); [Harrop & Turpin, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/13614533.2013.740961)). Some students walk down hallways pulling on doors at random—frequently interrupting active classes or settling into a room only to be displaced ten minutes later by an incoming lecture ([Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)). Others attempt to check official university portals, which fragment information across three disconnected pages: the [UBC Online Timetable](https://sws-van.as.it.ubc.ca/) (which requires CWL login and only displays schedules one single room at a time), the [UBC Building Hours directory](https://learningspaces.ubc.ca/find-a-space/), and the [Informal Learning Spaces directory](https://learningspaces.ubc.ca/find-a-space-informal/). None of these static, room-by-room interfaces allow students to query which rooms are free across a time window, nor do they account for walking travel times between buildings ([Harrop & Turpin, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/13614533.2013.740961)) or real-time conditions on the ground, such as locked doors, unscheduled club meetings, or rooms that are already full ([Azizi et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2020.107355)). Because these improvised methods require high effort for uncertain results, students remain bottlenecked in overcrowded libraries while viable classrooms down the hall sit unused ([Cha & Kim, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acalib.2015.03.014); [Beyrouthy et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1057/palgrave.jors.2602523)).
